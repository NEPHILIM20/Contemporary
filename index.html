Module 01

Core Concepts

Understanding the foundational vocabulary of Asian regionalism

🌏

Region

  • Groups of countries in the same geographically specified area
  • Defined by unique topographical features, economic activities, and historical contexts
  • Distinguished by dialects, social manners, and cultural identity
⚙️

Regionalization

  • Process of dividing an area into smaller segments called regions
  • Division of a nation into states or provinces
  • In economic context, serves as a Management Tool
🤝

Regionalism

  • Created as a sort of counter-globalization
  • Regional organizations prefer regional partners over the rest of the world
  • A political and economic philosophy favoring regional cooperation

Module 02

Globalization vs Regionalization

🌍 Globalization
🗺️ Regionalization
🌐Nature
Promotes integration of economies across state borders all around the world
Divides an area into smaller, more manageable segments
📈Market
Allows many corporations to trade internationally, enabling a free market
Monopolies are more likely to develop within a limited regional market
🎭Culture
Accelerates multiculturalism through free and inexpensive movement of people
Does not actively support multiculturalism across different areas
❤️Aid
Globalized communities are more willing to aid disaster-stricken countries
A regionalized area typically does not get involved in affairs of other areas
💻Technology
Globalization has driven great technological advances across borders
Advanced technology is rarely available within a single country or region

Module 03

History of Regionalization

in Southeast Asia

Post WWII

Birth of Cooperation

After the Great War, countries sought new ways to manage shared interests, threats, and opportunities.

Cold War Era

Marginalized Economies Unite

Marginalized economies collaborated with neighboring countries to gain better access to global markets.

1960s–1970s

New Regionalism Emerges

States, non-state actors, and ideological groups shaped regional cooperation driven by global changes.

August 8, 1967

ASEAN Founded

Five Southeast Asian nations formed ASEAN in Bangkok, Thailand — a landmark in regional cooperation.

Post-Cold War

Expansion & AFTA

ASEAN expanded its membership and created the ASEAN Free Trade Area (AFTA) to strengthen economic growth.

1997–1998

Asian Financial Crisis

Currency collapse and debt crises struck the region, prompting ASEAN to seek stronger financial cooperation.

Post-2001

Wider Regionalism

Following Lee Kuan Yew's vision, ASEAN shifted toward integrating with China and beyond — "Unity in Diversity."

Module 05

Challenges & Response

How ASEAN navigated crises, geopolitical pressure, and security threats

💸
1997–1998

Asian Financial Crisis

Currency collapse, economic instability, and debt crises struck many Asian economies.

Response

ASEAN shifted toward regional financial cooperation, reducing dependence on the IMF and the United States, developing Asian-led financial frameworks.

💣
2001 onward

Post-9/11 Geopolitics

The 2001 bombings shifted global focus toward a "War on Terror," placing Southeast Asia under high scrutiny.

Response

ASEAN acted with extreme caution to avoid provoking diverse local populations while cooperating on counter-terrorism efforts.

🏛️
Post-Cold War

US Unilateralism

Superpower pressure demanded compliance with Western security mandates, threatening regional autonomy.

Response

ASEAN pursued strategic multilateralism, engaging the EU, Russia, and the SCO to ensure no single superpower dictated regional affairs.

🔫
2000s

Regional Security Threats

The rise of regional terrorist networks forced a re-evaluation of internal defense and porous borders.

Response

ASEAN developed collaborative security frameworks and intelligence-sharing mechanisms across member states.

ASEAN's Strategic Pillars

🌐Open Regionalism

Engaging with partners outside the region while maintaining regional identity

🏳️Sovereignty

Preserving each nation's right to self-determination and governance

🤐Non-interference

Member states do not meddle in each other's domestic affairs

🗳️Consensus

All major decisions require agreement from all member states

Unity in Diversity

ASEAN's enduring vision — proving that cultural differences are a source of strength, not conflict, and that shared challenges are best met through collective action.
